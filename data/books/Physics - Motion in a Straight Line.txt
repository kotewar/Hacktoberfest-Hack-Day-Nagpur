Chapter 2: Motion in a Straight Line

2.1 Kinematic Basics:
Motion is change in position of an object with time. In order to specify position, we need to use a reference point and a set of axes.
- Path Length (Distance): Total distance traversed by an object. It is a scalar quantity and is always non-negative.
- Displacement: Change in position vector of the object (Final Position - Initial Position). It is a vector quantity and can be positive, zero, or negative.

2.2 Speed and Velocity:
- Average Speed: Total path length divided by the total time interval taken.
Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time (SI unit: m/s).
- Average Velocity: Displacement divided by the time interval.
Average Velocity = Delta x / Delta t.
- Instantaneous Velocity: The velocity at an instant is defined as the limit of the average velocity as the time interval Delta t becomes infinitesimally small:
v = lim (Delta t -> 0) [Delta x / Delta t] = dx/dt.

2.3 Acceleration:
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time.
Average acceleration = (v2 - v1) / (t2 - t1) = Delta v / Delta t.
Instantaneous acceleration a = dv/dt = d^2x/dt^2 (SI unit: m/s^2).
If velocity decreases with time, acceleration is negative (retardation or deceleration).

2.4 Kinematic Equations for Uniformly Accelerated Motion:
For an object moving with constant acceleration 'a', initial velocity 'u', final velocity 'v', time 't', and displacement 's':
1. First Equation of Motion:
   v = u + a * t
2. Second Equation of Motion:
   s = u * t + (1/2) * a * t^2
3. Third Equation of Motion:
   v^2 = u^2 + 2 * a * s
4. Displacement in n-th second:
   s_n = u + (a/2) * (2n - 1)

2.5 Free Fall under Gravity:
An object released near the surface of the earth accelerates downwards under the influence of gravity (g = 9.8 m/s^2 or ~10 m/s^2).
Neglecting air resistance:
- v = u - g * t
- y = u * t - (1/2) * g * t^2
- v^2 = u^2 - 2 * g * y
Maximum height reached: H_max = u^2 / (2 * g)
Time of flight: T = 2 * u / g
